Common Mistakes People Make When Searching For A Locksmith

Talk to an expert ↗(07) 5588 8111
SPL Security Solutions • August 21, 2026

A lockout, a break-in, or a security upgrade rarely happens at a convenient time — and that urgency is exactly what leads a lot of people to make a rushed, costly decision when searching for a locksmith. Here are the most common mistakes people make, and how to avoid them so you end up with a qualified, trustworthy professional instead of a headache.

Mistake #1: Choosing Based On Urgency Alone

When you're locked out or dealing with a security issue right now, it's tempting to call the very first result you see, without checking who you're actually inviting to work on your locks. This is understandable, but it's also the single biggest reason people end up with unqualified or even fraudulent operators.

Taking even thirty seconds to verify who you're calling — are they licensed, do they have a genuine local presence, do they show up in real reviews — makes a real difference to the outcome, even when you're in a genuine hurry.

Mistake #2: Trusting Unverified Online Listings

Not every listing that shows up under "commercial locksmith" or a similar search represents a genuine local business. Some directories and ad listings are run by call centres that simply collect your job and outsource it to whoever's available and cheapest, rather than a vetted, qualified locksmith with real accountability for the work.

This is one of the more common traps people fall into, particularly during an emergency search at odd hours. A genuine local locksmith should have a real, verifiable business presence — a proper website, a fixed local phone number, and a track record you can actually check, not just a generic ad.

Mistake #3: Not Checking Licensing Or Credentials

Locksmithing is a trade that genuinely benefits from formal qualifications, particularly for anything beyond a basic lockout. Choosing an operator with recognised industry credentials — ideally a Master Locksmith, and a member of a professional body such as the Master Locksmiths Association of Australasia (MLAA) or the Australian Security Industry Association Limited (ASIAL) — gives you a level of assurance that unlicensed or unaffiliated operators simply can't match.

It's also worth confirming the technician is police-checked, particularly for residential and commercial work where they'll have direct access to your locks, keys and security systems.

Mistake #4: Skipping Reviews, Guarantees And Response Times

A locksmith's reputation tells you a lot about what to actually expect. Skipping a quick check of reviews, workmanship guarantees, and stated response times can leave you exposed to poor-quality work, rushed jobs, or providers who simply don't show up when promised.

Look specifically for a workmanship guarantee, since a reputable locksmith should stand behind their work rather than leaving you to deal with a repeat callout. Genuine response time commitments, particularly for 24/7 emergency services, are also a good indicator of a business that's set up to actually deliver on urgent jobs, not just advertise them.

Mistake #5: Not Getting Transparent, Upfront Pricing

Hidden charges are one of the most common complaints against disreputable locksmiths, particularly those operating through outsourced call centres. A quoted callout fee that balloons once the technician arrives, undisclosed parts costs, or vague "from" pricing with no real detail are all red flags.

A trustworthy locksmith should be willing to give you a free, clear quote before committing to the job, so you know what you're paying and why, rather than being presented with a surprise invoice once the work is already done.

Mistake #6: Choosing A Generalist For A Specialised Job

Not every locksmith job is the same, and not every locksmith has equal experience across residential, commercial, and automotive work. A locksmith who's excellent at basic residential lockouts may not have the right experience or equipment for a complex commercial access control system, or vice versa.

If you need a commercial locksmith Brisbane and Gold Coast businesses can rely on for something like a master key system, keyless entry systems, or an integrated access control setup, it's worth confirming the provider genuinely specialises in that area, rather than assuming any locksmith can handle any job equally well.

The same logic applies to automotive locksmith work, which often requires specific tools and vehicle-specific knowledge that a general residential locksmith may not have.

How To Choose A Reputable Locksmith

Putting this all together, a few simple checks go a long way toward avoiding these common mistakes:

  • Confirm a genuine local presence — a real address, consistent local phone number, and an established track record in your area, rather than a nameless entry that only shows up in a "commercial locksmith" search.
  • Check for recognised credentials — Master Locksmith status and membership with bodies like MLAA or ASIAL are strong indicators of quality and accountability.
  • Ask for an upfront quote before committing to any work.
  • Look for a workmanship guarantee and clear information on response times.
  • Confirm proper identification on arrival — a legitimate technician should be happy to identify themselves and their business before starting work.
  • Match the provider to the job — residential, commercial and automotive locksmithing all benefit from specific experience, and if you're considering an upgrade like keyless entry systems, make sure the provider has genuine experience installing and supporting that specific technology.

Faqs

  • How can I tell if an online locksmith listing is legitimate?

    Check for a genuine local address, a consistent phone number used across multiple platforms, and real, verifiable reviews. Be cautious of listings with vague business details or generic, templated descriptions that could apply to any business anywhere.

  • What credentials should a trustworthy locksmith have?

    Look for recognised qualifications such as Master Locksmith status, along with membership in professional bodies like the Master Locksmiths Association of Australasia (MLAA) or the Australian Security Industry Association Limited (ASIAL). Police-checked technicians add an extra layer of assurance.

  • Why do some locksmiths charge more once they arrive than what was originally quoted?

    This often happens with unverified or outsourced providers who quote a low callout fee to secure the job, then add undisclosed charges once on-site. A reputable locksmith provides transparent, upfront pricing before work begins.

  • Is it worth choosing a specialist for commercial or automotive locksmith work?

    Yes. Commercial security systems — including keyless entry systems — and automotive key services often require specific tools, technical knowledge and experience that a general residential locksmith may not have. Confirming a provider's specific expertise before booking helps ensure the job is done properly.

  • What should I ask before booking an emergency locksmith?

    Ask about their licensing and credentials, whether they offer a workmanship guarantee, their estimated response time, and for a clear upfront quote. A legitimate provider should answer all of these confidently and without hesitation.
