As a building or business grows, managing keys gets complicated: more doors, more staff, more contractors and more keys to keep track of. A master key system replaces a ring of unrelated keys with one planned key hierarchy, so each person carries a single key that opens exactly what they're allowed to open.

How a master key system works

Every lock in the system is keyed so it can be opened by its own key and by the higher-level keys above it. A well-designed master key system gives you:

  • Tiered access across different areas, floors, tenancies or departments.
  • Fewer keys in circulation, because one key does the job of many.
  • Controlled access for staff, managers and contractors: managers can open all areas while staff access only designated zones.

Without professional planning, master key systems can become confusing or insecure. We design each system to balance access control with how your building actually runs, and we also offer keyed-alike options where one key should open a set of locks.

Key control with restricted keys

A master key is only as secure as the control over who can copy it. With a restricted master key system, keys and barrels use a registered or patented design that's only available to approved agents, so you won't find the blanks at the local hardware or key cutting shop.

  • Signatories control the system. You nominate the people authorised to order keys or work, such as owners, managers, caretakers or the body corporate, and their details are kept on file.
  • No unauthorised copies. We will not cut a duplicate key without a letter or order signed by one of the signatories for your system.
  • Every key is traceable. Restricted keys are individually stamped with the system number, rank and sequential generation, so you know how many keys are in circulation at any one time.

For sites that need the highest level of key security, ask us about high-security restricted systems such as Abloy Protec, which is used extensively by private, commercial and government end users.

Keys and access control together

Many buildings use both. Master keys suit doors that rarely change users, while electronic access control lets you change permissions without rekeying and add doors in software rather than re-issuing master keys. We can plan the two together so high-traffic or sensitive doors are on cards, fobs or codes and the rest of the building stays on a well-managed key system.

Frequently asked questions

What is a master key system?

A master key system is a planned hierarchy of keys and locks. Each person's key opens only the doors they need, while managers or building staff hold higher-level keys that open more areas. It means tiered access across different areas, fewer keys in circulation and controlled access for staff, managers and contractors.

Who is a master key system suited to?

Any building where different people need different levels of access: strata and body corporate complexes, offices and multi-tenant buildings, schools and other institutions, retail centres and commercial or industrial premises.

Can our master keys be protected from copying?

Yes. A restricted master key system uses patent-protected or registered keys that are only available to approved agents, so they can't be copied at a hardware store or key cutting kiosk. We will not cut a duplicate key without a letter or order signed by one of the signatories for your system.

Who can order extra keys for our system?

Only the signatories listed for the system. When the system is set up, you complete a registration form naming the people authorised to order keys or work, such as owners, managers, caretakers or the body corporate. Signatories can be changed by submitting a new Signatory Registration Form.

What should we do if a master key is lost?

Call us on (07) 55 888 111 (24/7), or (02) 6686 7675 in Northern NSW. We'll talk you through the options, which can include rekeying the affected locks so the lost key no longer works.

Can you take over or extend our existing system?

Yes. We service, repair and maintain locks and locking systems, including those installed by other companies. A restricted system can be transferred between locksmiths if the receiving locksmith has, or can get, the key profile the system is on; systems on an exclusive profile may need to be arranged through the manufacturer.
